03

Frequently asked questions

The questions every algebra student asks about linear equations — answered with worked numbers.

Q1How do I solve a linear equation step by step?
Keep both sides balanced — whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Expand parentheses, combine like terms, move every variable term to one side, move the constants to the other, then divide by the variable's coefficient. For 3(x − 4) = 2x + 1: distribute to 3x − 12 = 2x + 1, subtract 2x to get x − 12 = 1, then add 12 to get x = 13. The solver writes exactly these lines.
Q2How do I solve an equation with parentheses?
Distribute first: multiply the factor outside the bracket into every term inside. 3(x − 4) = 2x + 1 becomes 3x − 12 = 2x + 1, and from there it is an ordinary two-sided equation. The solver displays the fully distributed line before it begins collecting terms.
Q3How do I solve equations with the variable on both sides?
Collect the variable terms. In 5x − 7 = 3x + 9, subtract 3x from both sides to get 2x − 7 = 9, add 7 to get 2x = 16, and divide by 2: x = 8. Try the 5x − 7 = 3x + 9 chip on the tool to see it worked.
Q4How do I solve a linear equation with fractions or decimals?
Treat them as exact numbers. x/2 + 1 = x/3 becomes ½x + 1 = ⅓x; collecting gives (½ − ⅓)x = −1, so ⅙x = −1 and x = −6. Decimals are converted to fractions the moment you type them — 0.5 is stored as 1/2 — so nothing is ever rounded mid-solution.
Q5What makes an equation linear?
A linear equation in one variable can be written ax + b = 0 with a ≠ 0: the variable has exponent 1, is never multiplied by itself, and never sits in a denominator. x² + 1 = 5 is quadratic; 6/x = 2 is not linear in x. This solver handles degree exactly 1 — and tells you plainly when something isn't.
Q6When does a linear equation have no solution — or infinitely many?
When the variable cancels out. 2x + 5 = 2x + 9 collapses to 5 = 9 — false for every x, so no solution. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 collapses to 0 = 0 — true for every x, an identity with infinitely many solutions. The solver announces which of the three verdicts (one solution, none, all reals) your equation earns.
Q7How do I solve a system of two linear equations?
By elimination: scale the equations until one variable's coefficients match, subtract to eliminate it, solve the remaining single-variable equation, then back-substitute. For ① x + y = 6 and ② x − y = 2, adding eliminates y: 2x = 8, so x = 4 and y = 2. The SYSTEM tab performs and displays each of these moves.
Q8Is it free, and does my equation leave the page?
Completely free — no sign-up, no locked steps. All parsing and arithmetic run locally in your browser; nothing is recorded or transmitted, and once the page has loaded it keeps working offline.
