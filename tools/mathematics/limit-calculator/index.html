03

Frequently asked questions

The questions every calculus student asks about limits — answered with worked numbers.

Q1How do I solve a limit step by step?
Try direct substitution first — if f(c) is defined, the limit is f(c). If you get 0/0, factor the numerator and denominator, cancel the common (x − c) factor, and substitute again. For trig and exponential forms use the standard equivalences; at infinity compare degrees; otherwise build a table of values. For (x² − 9)/(x − 3) at 3: substitution gives 0/0, factor to (x − 3)(x + 3)/(x − 3), cancel to x + 3, substitute to get 6. The calculator writes exactly these lines.
Q2What does lim x→c f(x) = L actually mean?
The ε–δ idea: f(x) can be forced as close to L as anyone demands, just by taking x close enough to c — from both sides, but never at c. The value f(c) is irrelevant to the limit and may not even exist. That is why (x² − 9)/(x − 3) has limit 6 at x = 3 even though the fraction is undefined exactly there.
Q3What is the 0/0 indeterminate form?
When substitution makes both the numerator and the denominator zero, the limit is genuinely hidden — 0/0 can equal anything: x/x → 1, x²/x → 0, sin(3x)/x → 3, (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2. Indeterminate means the answer is decided by how fast each side approaches zero, not that no answer exists. Factor and cancel, or use the standard equivalences.
Q4How do I evaluate limits at infinity?
For rational functions use the degree rules: equal degrees → the ratio of leading coefficients, so (3x² + 1)/(5x² − 2) → 3/5; smaller degree on top → 0; bigger degree on top → ±∞. In general, divide everything by the highest power of x and let every 1/x term die. The tool displays this divide-by-xⁿ move line by line.
Q5When does a limit not exist?
Three classic ways: the two sides disagree — 1/x at 0 goes to −∞ from the left and +∞ from the right; unbounded growth — 1/x² at 0 blows up to +∞ (usually reported as an infinite limit rather than a failure); and oscillation — sin(1/x) at 0 never settles on any value. The solver announces which of the three verdicts your limit earns, with the evidence.
Q6What are the standard limit equivalences?
As u → 0: sin u ~ u, tan u ~ u, 1 − cos u ~ u²/2, e^u − 1 ~ u, and ln(1 + u) ~ u. Each means the ratio of the two sides tends to 1, so you may swap them in a quotient. Example: sin(3x)/x → 3x/x → 3. The solver applies exactly these swaps, and shows each one before continuing.
Q7How do one-sided limits work?
x → c⁻ approaches c from below, x → c⁺ from above. The two-sided limit exists only when both one-sided limits exist and agree. For √x at 0 the left-hand limit does not exist (the function is undefined for x < 0), so the two-sided limit fails while the right-hand limit is 0. Use the LEFT and RIGHT modes of the tool for these.
Q8What about L'Hôpital's rule?
For genuine 0/0 or ∞/∞ forms you may differentiate the numerator and denominator separately and retry. It is powerful, but the algebraic routes — factor and cancel, the equivalence toolbox, conjugates, degree rules — usually come first in class, and that is the order this solver follows. The numeric table serves as ground truth either way.

Completely free — no sign-up, no locked steps. All parsing and arithmetic run locally in your browser; nothing is recorded or transmitted, and the page keeps working offline once loaded.
