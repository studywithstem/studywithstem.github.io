    03

    Frequently asked.

    The questions that keep coming back about percentages — answered once, in full.

    Q1 How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

    Turn the percent into a decimal (divide it by 100) and multiply. For 15% of 200: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15, then 0.15 × 200 = 30. The FIND tab does exactly this and shows every line.

    Q2 How do I calculate what percent one number is of another?

    Part over whole, times 100. For 30 is what percent of 200: 30 ÷ 200 = 0.15, and 0.15 × 100 = 15%. The IS tab writes these steps live.

    Q3 How do I calculate percentage increase or decrease?

    Change divided by the original, times 100. From 40 to 50: the change is +10, the original is 40, so 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25% — a 25% increase. A negative result means a decrease. The CHANGE tab measures and checks both.

    Q4 How do I add or subtract a percentage from a number?

    Find the percentage of the number, then add or subtract it: 200 + 15% means 15% of 200 = 30, then 200 + 30 = 230. Shortcut: multiply by 1.15 (or 0.85 for minus). The ADJUST tab shows both routes, every time.

    Q5 Why does percent change divide by the original value?

    Because percent means "per hundred of something" — and that something is where you started. The same distance gives different percentages from different starting points: 40 → 50 is a 25% increase, but 50 → 40 is only a 20% decrease. Same 10, different base.

    Q6 What is the difference between percent change and percent difference?

    Percent change is directional — it measures the new value against the old one, so order matters. Percent difference is symmetric — it measures two values against their average, and is used when neither is "the original", such as comparing two experimental results. This tool computes percent change.

    Q7 What does the line over some decimals mean?

    It marks a repeating decimal: 33.3 with a bar over the final 3 means 33.333…%, forever, with no rounding. Results like 1 ÷ 3 never terminate, so the calculator shows the exact repeating block instead of pretending it's 33.33.

    Q8 Is it free, and does my input leave the page?

    Completely free — no sign-up, no locked features. All arithmetic runs locally in your browser; nothing is recorded or transmitted, and the page keeps working offline once it has loaded.
