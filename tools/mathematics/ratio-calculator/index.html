FIELD NOTESRT-01

  • A ratio is a comparison. 2 : 3 means two of the first for every three of the second. Order is part of the meaning — 3 : 2 is a different ratio.
  • Proportion means two equal ratios. a : b = c : d is true exactly when the cross products match: a·d = b·c. That one rule cracks every missing-value problem.
  • Scale, don't stretch. Multiply or divide every term by the same number and nothing changes: 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 40 : 60. Simplifying is just scaling down by the gcd.
  • Part-to-part vs part-to-whole. In 2 : 3 the parts are 2 and 3, the whole is 5 — so the shares of the whole are 2/5 and 3/5, that is 40% and 60%.
  • Decimals clear out. 1.5 : 2 becomes 15 : 20 (×10) before Euclid runs — the arithmetic never rounds, not once.
  • Famous ratios. Golden 1 : 1.618…, A4 paper 1 : √2, widescreen 16 : 9, Pythagoras 3 : 4 : 5.

03

Questions, answered

The ratio questions that actually come up — short answers, no fluff.

Q01How do I solve a ratio with a missing value?
Write it as a proportion and cross-multiply. For 3:4 = x:12, read it as 3/4 = x/12, so 3 × 12 = 4 × x, giving 36 = 4x and x = 9. The SOLVE mode does this for a blank in any of the four positions and writes each line out as it goes.
Q02How do I simplify a ratio to lowest terms?
Divide every term by the greatest common divisor. For 16:12, Euclid's algorithm gives gcd = 4, so 16÷4 : 12÷4 = 4:3. The SIMPLIFY tab runs Euclid line by line so you can copy the working straight into your notes.
Q03How do I simplify a ratio with three terms?
The same rule, applied to all terms at once — the gcd of three numbers is gcd(gcd(a, b), c). For 45:60:75 that is gcd(15, 75) = 15, giving 3:4:5, the classic Pythagorean triple. The third box in SIMPLIFY mode is optional: leave it empty for two-term ratios.
Q04How do I divide an amount in a given ratio?
Add the terms to find the total parts, divide the amount by that total to get one part, then multiply by each term. $120 in 2:3 gives 5 parts, one part = 24, and shares of 48 and 72. The SHARE mode finishes by checking that the shares add back to the amount.
Q05What is the difference between part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios?
2:3 is a part-to-part comparison — two quantities set against each other. The whole is 2 + 3 = 5 parts, so the same information part-to-whole is 2/5 and 3/5, or 40% and 60%. The rows under the screen show both readings.
Q06How do I convert a ratio to a fraction, decimal or percentage?
Part-to-part, a:b is simply the fraction a/b: 3:4 = 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%, with repeating decimals shown exactly under an overline. Part-to-whole puts the total underneath instead: 3:4 becomes 3/7 and 4/7, about 42.9% and 57.1%.
Q07Can a ratio contain decimals, fractions or units?
Yes. Clear decimals by scaling: 1.5:2 → ×10 → 15:20 → 3:4. Units are fine as long as both sides use the same one — 250 g flour to 500 g water is just 1:2. Only a zero in the second term of a ratio used as a fraction is forbidden.
Q08What is the golden ratio?
Two quantities are in the golden ratio when the whole is to the larger part as the larger is to the smaller: (a+b):a = a:b. It equals φ ≈ 1.6180339…, and 1:φ is the only ratio that reproduces itself under that split.
Q09Is it free, and does my input leave the page?
Completely free — no sign-up, no locked features. All arithmetic runs locally in your browser; nothing is recorded or transmitted, and the page keeps working offline once it has loaded.
