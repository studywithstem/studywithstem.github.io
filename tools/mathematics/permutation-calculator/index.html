        THE MATH
        
One idea, four shapes.

        
A permutation counts ordered arrangements. The only question is what your items are allowed to do — match your problem to one of these four shapes.

      

      

        

          01 — NO REPETITION
          
Distinct items, used once

          
P(n, r) = n! / (n − r)!

          
Order matters and each item appears at most once — you're choosing r out of n and lining them up.

          
Podium from 9 runners: P(9, 3) = 9 × 8 × 7 = 504

        

        

          02 — WITH REPETITION
          
Every slot can reuse any item

          
n^r

          
Each of the r positions independently picks from the n items — like digits in a PIN code.

          
4-digit PIN: 104 = 10,000 codes

        

        

          03 — CIRCULAR
          
Around a circle, rotations equal

          
(n − 1)!

          
Fix one item to cancel rotational duplicates, then arrange the remaining n − 1 freely.

          
6 people at a table: 5! = 120 seatings

        

        

          04 — REPEATED ITEMS
          
Identical items merge

          
n! / (a! × b! × …)

          
When some items are indistinguishable, divide out the internal orderings of each repeated group.

          
BANANA: 6! / (3! × 2!) = 60

        

      

      

        ORDER DOESN'T MATTER? Then you need a combination, not a permutation: C(n, r) = P(n, r) / r!. The same five items give P(5, 2) = 20 ordered pairs but only C(5, 2) = 10 unordered pairs — the r! orderings of each chosen group collapse into one.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      

        FAQ
        
Permutation questions, answered.

        
The eight questions that come up again and again when arranging things.

      

      

        

          What is a permutation?+
          
A permutation is an ordered arrangement of items — a selection where the sequence matters. Choosing gold, silver and bronze from nine runners is a permutation: 9 × 8 × 7 = 504 possible podiums. Choosing any three of the nine for a photo, ignoring order, would be a combination instead.

        

        

          What's the difference between a permutation and a combination?+
          
Order. If AB and BA count as different outcomes, it's a permutation — use P(n, r) = n!/(n−r)!. If they count as the same, it's a combination — C(n, r) = n!/(r!(n−r)!), which is always smaller because the r! orderings of each chosen group collapse into one. For example, P(5, 2) = 20 but C(5, 2) = 10.

        

        

          What does nPr mean on a calculator?+
          
nPr is the standard notation — and the button on scientific calculators — for permutations of n items taken r at a time: nPr = n!/(n−r)!. For example, 10P3 = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720. This tool computes the same thing, plus repetition, circular and word modes the calculator button doesn't have.

        

        

          How do I calculate permutations with repetition allowed?+
          
When each position can hold any of the n items — digits in a PIN, draws with replacement — the count is n to the power r. Ten digits across four positions gives 104 = 10,000 possible PINs. Use the “With repetition” mode above.

        

        

          What is a circular permutation?+
          
An arrangement around a circle, where rotating the whole ring doesn't create a new outcome. Fix one item in place to cancel rotations, then arrange the remaining n − 1 items: the count is (n − 1)!. Six people around a table gives 5! = 120 seatings. If mirror images also count as the same (a necklace), it's (n − 1)!/2.

        

        

          Why is 0! equal to 1?+
          
It's the empty product — there is exactly one way to arrange nothing. The convention keeps every formula working: P(n, n) = n!/0! = n!, and P(5, 0) = 1, since there is exactly one empty arrangement.

        

        

          How large can the numbers get?+
          
Inputs go up to n = 1000. Results use exact big-integer arithmetic rather than floating point, so every digit is correct — 1000! runs to 2,568 digits and the calculator shows all of them, with scientific notation alongside for readability.

        

        

          Does the calculator work offline and on mobile?+
          
Yes. It's fully responsive for phone, tablet and desktop, and all of the math runs locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded, and once the page has loaded it keeps working even if you go offline.
