    03

    Frequently asked.

    The questions that keep coming back about determinants — answered once, in full.

    Q1 What is a determinant, exactly?

    A determinant is a single number computed from a square matrix. Geometrically, it is the signed scale factor of the transformation the matrix represents: for a 2×2 it is the factor by which area stretches (the sign says whether space flips), for a 3×3 it is volume. Algebraically it decides whether a matrix can be inverted — and it appears throughout Cramer's rule and eigenvalue equations.

    Q2 How do I calculate the determinant of a 2×2 matrix?

    Multiply down the main diagonal, subtract the anti-diagonal: det = ad − bc. For [[3, 8], [4, 6]]: 3×6 − 8×4 = 18 − 32 = −14. The tool shows both diagonal products and the subtraction, every time.

    Q3 How do I calculate the determinant of a 3×3 matrix?

    Cofactor expansion along a row: det = a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg), where each bracketed pair is the 2×2 minor left after crossing out that entry's row and column. Pick the row or column with the most zeros to multiply less. Sarrus' rule — copy the first two columns to the right, add the ↘ products, subtract the ↗ ones — is a handy cross-check the tool runs automatically.

    Q4 How do I calculate the determinant of a 4×4 matrix?

    The same way, one level up: expand along the row or column with the most zeros, which reduces the 4×4 to a signed sum of 3×3 minors, then evaluate each 3×3 by its own expansion. The tool does exactly this — it scans all eight lines, picks the smartest, and shows every minor, its value, and the signed sum.

    Q5 What does it mean when the determinant is 0?

    The matrix is singular: it has no inverse, its rows (and columns) are linearly dependent — one is a combination of the others — and geometrically the transformation squashes space into a lower dimension. A matrix like [[1,2,3],[2,4,6],[7,8,9]], where row 2 is twice row 1, cannot be undone. Try the SINGULAR example chip.

    Q6 Can a determinant be negative?

    Yes. The magnitude is the scale factor — how much area or volume grows — and the sign records orientation: a negative determinant means the transformation flips space, like a mirror. In 2D, a negative determinant swaps clockwise for counterclockwise.

    Q7 What determinant rules should I know?

    The essentials: swapping two rows flips the sign; scaling a row scales the determinant; adding a multiple of one row to another leaves it unchanged; two identical rows force det = 0; triangular matrices have det equal to the product of the diagonal; det(I) = 1; det(AB) = det(A)·det(B); and det(Aᵀ) = det(A).

    Q8 Does it handle decimals, and is it free?

    Yes to both. Decimal entries become exact fractions internally — 0.1 is stored as 1/10 — so results never drift, and fractional results display as both an exact fraction and a decimal. The tool is completely free, requires no sign-up, runs entirely in your browser, and keeps working offline once loaded.
