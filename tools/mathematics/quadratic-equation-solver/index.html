FIELD NOTESΔ = b²−4ac

  • Δ > 0 — two distinct real roots; the parabola cuts the x-axis twice. Rational if Δ is a perfect square, surds otherwise.
  • Δ = 0 — one repeated root; the vertex rests exactly on the axis — a perfect-square trinomial.
  • Δ < 0 — a conjugate pair p ± qi; the curve misses the axis entirely.
  • Vieta's shortcut — sum of roots = −b/a, product = c/a. Read them before you solve; use them after, to check.
  • Why the formula works — complete the square on ax² + bx, balance both sides, and x = (−b ± √Δ)/2a falls out. Every run of this tool is that derivation with your numbers.
  • Exactness rule — integer coefficients stay integers to the last line; radicals are simplified (2√3, never √12) and fractions reduced.

03

Questions, answered

The discriminant, the formula, complex roots, Vieta's checks — the quadratic, from every angle.

Q.01What is the quadratic formula?
x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a solves every quadratic ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. It comes from completing the square once, symbolically — so it works even when factoring fails. This tool substitutes your a, b, c and writes out each line of the result.
Q.02What does the discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac tell me?
Its sign predicts the roots: Δ > 0 means two distinct real roots (the parabola crosses the x-axis twice); Δ = 0 means one repeated root with the vertex on the axis; Δ < 0 means no real roots — a complex conjugate pair. If Δ is positive and a perfect square (with integer coefficients), both roots are rational, which is exactly when factoring is easy.
Q.03How do I solve a quadratic by factoring?
Find two numbers that multiply to a·c and add to b. For x² − 5x + 6 the numbers are −2 and −3, giving (x − 2)(x − 3) and roots x = 2, 3. Factoring only works cleanly when the roots are rational; otherwise the quadratic formula is the way. The solver displays the factored form automatically whenever it exists.
Q.04What are complex roots — what if Δ is negative?
Taking the square root of a negative number introduces i, where i² = −1. The roots then come as a conjugate pair p ± qi: for example x² + x + 1 = 0 gives x = −1/2 ± (√3/2)i. Geometrically, the parabola never touches the x-axis. The solver keeps the exact surd part of the imaginary component — no rounding.
Q.05How do I find the vertex and axis of symmetry?
The vertex is at h = −b/2a, k = −Δ/4a, and the axis of symmetry is the vertical line x = h. If a > 0 the parabola opens upward and the vertex is the minimum; if a < 0 it opens downward and the vertex is the maximum. The GRAPH tab marks all of these live.
Q.06What happens if a = 0?
The equation is not quadratic — it becomes the line bx + c = 0 with root x = −c/b. The solver detects this, labels the equation linear and solves it anyway. If b is also 0, it tells you whether there is no solution (0 = c ≠ 0) or every x is a solution (0 = 0).
Q.07What are Vieta's formulas?
x₁ + x₂ = −b/a and x₁·x₂ = c/a. They hold exactly — even for surd and complex roots, where the radical parts cancel. They are a fast sanity check: x² − 5x + 6 must have roots summing to 5 and multiplying to 6, so 2 and 3.
Q.08Is the tool free, exact and private?
Completely free, no sign-up. Integer coefficients are solved with integer arithmetic — fractions reduced, radicals simplified, no rounding anywhere. Decimal inputs are computed to 9 significant figures and marked with ≈. Everything runs locally in your browser, and the page keeps working offline once loaded.
