    How to use it.

    Ninety seconds to full fluency — the notation is just boxes and a big answer. The tool does the showing-off for you.

    STEP 01

    Type the fractions.

    Each slot is three boxes — whole (optional), numerator, denominator. Type 2, 3, 4 and you've entered 2¾. Empty slots count as 0, and the arrow keys hop between top and bottom.

    2 · 3/4 → 2¾

    STEP 02

    Pick the operation.

    + − × ÷ sits between the slots. Addition and subtraction switch to the LCD method; multiplication goes straight across; division runs keep–change–flip. The tool follows the method you were taught.

    ÷ → KEEP · CHANGE · FLIP

    STEP 03

    No equals needed.

    The screen solves live as you type. The big line is the answer in its friendliest form — and the work beneath it is the full written proof, updating keystroke by keystroke.

    Frequently asked.

    The questions that keep coming back about fractions — answered once, in full.

    Q1 How do I add fractions with different denominators?

    Find the least common denominator (LCD), rewrite both fractions over it, then add the numerators. For 1/2 + 1/3: LCD(2, 3) = 6, so 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. The calculator performs and displays these exact steps under the screen, live as you type.

    Q2 How do I subtract fractions?

    Exactly like addition — match the denominators first using the LCD, then subtract the numerators. 7/8 − 1/4 becomes 7/8 − 2/8 = 5/8. Try the 7/8 − 1/4 example chip on the tool and follow the work.

    Q3 How do I multiply fractions?

    Multiply straight across — numerators with numerators, denominators with denominators. No common denominator needed. Then simplify: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12, which reduces to 1/2 by dividing top and bottom by their gcd.

    Q4 How do I divide fractions?

    Keep, change, flip: keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction to its reciprocal, then multiply. 3/4 ÷ 5/6 = 3/4 × 6/5 = 18/20 = 9/10. The tool writes out all of it, every time.

    Q5 How do I simplify a fraction to lowest terms?

    Divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor. For 46/64, Euclid's algorithm gives gcd = 2, so 46÷2 / 64÷2 = 23/32, which shares no factor besides 1. The SIMPLIFY tab does this for any fraction or mixed number — and shows the algorithm, line by line.

    Q6 What's the difference between proper, improper and mixed fractions?

    A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator (3/4 — value below 1). An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator (5/4 — value of 1 or more). A mixed number is a whole number plus a fraction (1 1/4) — just another way of writing the improper form. The tool displays your result as both.

    Q7 What does the line over some decimals mean?

    It marks a repeating decimal: 0.16 with a bar over the 6 means 0.1666…, forever. Fractions whose denominator (in lowest terms) has prime factors other than 2 and 5 always repeat. The calculator detects the repeating block by long division — no rounding — so 1/6 displays exactly as 0.16 with a bar, not 0.166667.

    Q8 Is it free, and does my input leave the page?

    Completely free — no sign-up, no locked features. All arithmetic runs locally in your browser; nothing is recorded or transmitted, and the page keeps working offline once it has loaded.
