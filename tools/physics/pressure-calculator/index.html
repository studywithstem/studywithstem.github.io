03 · THE PHYSICS

The formulas behind the numbers.

Every result the calculator shows is one of these lines doing the work — here they are, so you can check it by hand.

Contact pressure

P = F ÷ A

The defining formula: force perpendicular to a surface, divided by the area it's spread over. Newtons over square metres give pascals — halve the area and you double the pressure.

Hydrostatic pressure

P = ρ × g × h

The pressure a liquid column of density ρ and height h exerts at its base. Fresh water adds 9.81 kPa for every metre of depth — roughly one atmosphere per 10 m.

Gauge vs absolute

P(abs) = P(atm) + ρgh

Gauge pressure reads zero in open air. Add the atmosphere's 101.325 kPa to get absolute pressure — the number that matters for physics, not just for tire gauges.

Ideal gas law

P × V = n × R × T

Rearranged, pressure = nRT ÷ V. Temperature must be kelvin, volume cubic metres, and R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) — then 1 mol at 0 °C in 22.414 L lands on one atmosphere.

The pascal

1 Pa = 1 N/m²

One newton spread over one square metre. It's tiny — a sheet of paper resting on a table presses with about 1 Pa — which is why kPa, bar and psi do most of the everyday work.

Boyle's law

P₁ × V₁ = P₂ × V₂

Squeeze a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature and pressure trades with volume: half the volume, double the pressure. It's the ideal gas law with n and T held still.

04 · FAQ

Pressure, answered.

The questions that come up most when pressure homework stops cooperating. If yours isn't here, the formulas section above — or the calculator itself — probably covers it.

What is the formula for pressure?+

Pressure is force spread over area: P = F ÷ A. Take the force acting perpendicular to a surface, divide by the area it acts on, and the answer is in pascals — the SI unit defined as exactly 1 N/m².

Example: 500 N resting on 0.05 m² of table gives P = 500 ÷ 0.05 = 10,000 Pa = 10 kPa.

How do I calculate pressure from force and area?+

Measure or estimate the force in newtons and the contact area in square metres, then divide. A 500 N person stepping on a 1 cm² (0.0001 m²) high-heel tip produces 5,000,000 Pa ≈ 725 psi, while the same force on a 0.07 m² elephant foot gives only about 214,000 Pa — same force, very different pressure.

Mode 01 of the calculator does this, and can also reverse-solve for force or area.

What is the difference between gauge and absolute pressure?+

Gauge pressure is measured relative to the atmosphere, so it reads zero at sea level. Absolute pressure is measured from a perfect vacuum: P(abs) = P(gauge) + 101.325 kPa.

A tire at 32 psi gauge is about 46.7 psi absolute. Mode 02 has a one-tap switch between the two.

How do I calculate water pressure at depth?+

Use the hydrostatic formula P = ρgh, where ρ is density (about 1000 kg/m³ for fresh water, 1025 for sea water), g is 9.81 m/s² and h is depth in metres.

Every 10 m of fresh water adds roughly 98.1 kPa ≈ 1 atmosphere — a handy rule of thumb. At 40 m, a diver feels about 392 kPa gauge from the water alone.

Which pressure units does the converter support?+

Pascals (Pa), hectopascals, kilopascals, megapascals, gigapascals, bar, millibar, atmospheres, psi, ksi, psf, mmHg (torr), inHg, mmH₂O and mH₂O.

Every conversion goes through pascals: 1 atm = 101,325 Pa exactly, 1 bar = 100,000 Pa exactly, and 1 psi = 6,894.757293 Pa.

How does the ideal gas pressure calculator work?+

It rearranges PV = nRT to P = nRT ÷ V. Enter the amount of gas in moles, the volume and the temperature; the tool converts temperature to kelvin (K = °C + 273.15) and volume to cubic metres, then uses R = 8.314462618 J/(mol·K).

One mole of gas at 0 °C in 22.414 L comes out at 101,326 Pa — essentially one atmosphere.

Why does a smaller contact area increase pressure?+

Because P = F ÷ A: the same force divided by less area gives a bigger result. That's why needles pierce, knives cut, and snowshoes keep you on top of the snow — spreading your weight over more area lowers the pressure.

Doubling the contact area halves the pressure, and vice versa.

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
