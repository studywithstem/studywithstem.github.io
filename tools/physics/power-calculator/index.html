03 · THE MATH

The laws behind the buttons.

Six rules cover almost every power you will ever meet — the calculator applies all of them for you.

The basic power

bⁿ = b × b × ⋯ × b  (n factors)

The exponent counts how many copies of the base get multiplied. 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

The zero power

b⁰ = 1

Multiply zero copies of the base and you are left with the empty product — 1. It holds for every non-zero base.

Negative powers

b⁻ⁿ = 1 / bⁿ

A negative exponent flips the base into its reciprocal: 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8 = 0.125. The result is small, not negative.

Fractional powers

b^(p/q) = ( q√b )^p

A fractional exponent is a root in disguise: 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 2² = 4, and 9^0.5 is simply √9 = 3.

Roots as powers

ⁿ√x = x^(1/n)

Every root is a power with a reciprocal exponent. ∛64 = 64^(1/3) = 4, because 4³ = 64.

Combining powers

bᵐ · bⁿ = b^(m+n)  ·  (bᵐ)ⁿ = b^(mn)

Multiply same-base powers by adding exponents; raise a power to a power by multiplying them. 2³ · 2² = 2⁵ = 32.

04 · FAQ

Questions, answered.

The eight things people ask most about powers, roots and this calculator — answered plainly.

How do I calculate the power of a number?+

Multiply the base by itself as many times as the exponent says: bⁿ = b × b × ⋯ × b with n factors. For example 2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16. Type the base and exponent into the Power tab and the calculator handles the rest — including negative, fractional and very large exponents.

What does a negative exponent mean?+

A negative exponent means "take the reciprocal": b⁻ⁿ = 1 / bⁿ. So 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8 = 0.125. The result is not negative — it is a fraction. The calculator shows the exact fraction and the decimal side by side.

Why is anything to the power of 0 equal to 1?+

Each time the exponent rises by 1 you multiply by the base once more; each time it falls by 1 you divide by the base. Stepping from b¹ down to b⁰ divides by b once, leaving 1 — true for every non-zero base.

0⁰ is the famous exception: undefined in pure physics, but taken as 1 by convention in almost all textbooks and calculators, including this one.

How are fractional exponents and roots related?+

A fractional exponent is a root in disguise: b^(1/n) is the n-th root of b, and b^(p/q) = (ⁿ√b)^p. For example 9^0.5 = √9 = 3 and 8^(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4.

Enter 0.5 as an exponent for a square root, 0.25 for a fourth root — or simply use the Root tab.

How does the calculator show huge powers like 2¹⁰⁰⁰ exactly?+

When the base is an integer and the exponent is a whole number, the tool switches to arbitrary-precision arithmetic and prints every digit — 2¹⁰⁰⁰ has 302 of them. Scientific notation is shown alongside, and the full exact value can be copied with one click.

Why does a negative base with a fractional exponent give an error?+

Because the result is not a real number. An even root of a negative value requires complex numbers: (−4)^0.5 = 2i. Odd roots are fine — (−8)^(1/3) = −2 — and the calculator does compute them whenever the denominator of the exponent is odd.

What is the difference between the base, the exponent and the power?+

In bⁿ, the base b is the number being multiplied, the exponent n counts how many times it is multiplied, and the power is the result. In 2⁵ = 32: base 2, exponent 5, power 32. In everyday speech "power" is often used for the exponent too — context makes it clear.

Is this calculator free, and is my data sent anywhere?+

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
