03 · THE FORMULAS

Every mode, one line each.

The four formulas this calculator runs on — plus two rearrangements you’ll meet in homework.

Work from force

W = F · d · cos θ

Force × displacement × the cosine of the angle between them. A 150 N push along 8 m of floor does 1,200 J.

Work-energy theorem

W = ½m(v₂² − v₁²)

Net work equals the change in kinetic energy — speeding up is positive work, braking is negative.

Lifting work

W = m · g · h

Raising 10 kg by 2 m on Earth takes 10 × 9.81 × 2 ≈ 196 J — exactly the potential energy gained.

Power

P = W / t

Work per unit of time, in watts. Delivering 1,000 J in 10 s means 100 W of power.

Solving for force

F = W / (d · cos θ)

The rearrangement for when the work is known — say, putting 600 J into a 4 m push at 0°.

The joule

1 J = 1 N·m = 1 W·s

One joule is a one-newton push over one metre — roughly lifting an apple up to table height.

04 · FAQ

Work, minus the confusion.

The questions that come up in every physics class, answered in plain language with the numbers to back them up.

What is the formula for work done?+

Work equals force × displacement × the cosine of the angle between them: W = F · d · cos θ. Pushing a box with 150 N across 8 m of floor (force straight along the motion, θ = 0°) does W = 150 × 8 × 1 = 1,200 J. Whenever force is in newtons and distance in metres, work comes out in joules.

When is work done equal to zero?+

Three ways: the force is zero, the displacement is zero, or the force is perpendicular to the motion (θ = 90° or 270°, where cos θ = 0). The last one is the classic trap — carrying a heavy bag horizontally at constant speed does zero work on it, because the upward supporting force never acts along the horizontal motion.

Why does the work formula include cos θ?+

Only the component of the force that points along the displacement does any work, and that component is F · cos θ. Pulling a sled with a rope angled 30° above the ground, only cos 30° ≈ 0.866 of the pull moves the sled forward — the rest just lifts slightly. Enter the angle and the calculator applies the cosine automatically.

Can work be negative?+

Yes. Whenever the force has a component opposing the motion (90° < θ < 270°), cos θ is negative and so is the work. Friction acting on a sliding box does negative work, and so do brakes — they remove kinetic energy instead of adding it. Negative work is how moving things slow down.

What is the work-energy theorem?+

The total (net) work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy: W = ½m·v₂² − ½m·v₁². Speeding a 1,200 kg car from rest to 20 m/s takes ½ × 1200 × 20² = 240,000 J of work; braking it back to rest gives net work of −240,000 J.

How do I calculate the work done against gravity?+

Use W = m · g · h — mass × gravitational acceleration × height. Lifting 10 kg through 2 m on Earth takes 10 × 9.81 × 2 ≈ 196 J, exactly the gravitational potential energy gained. Lowering the same mass gives the same amount back as negative work.

What is the difference between work and power?+

Work is an amount of energy transferred, measured in joules; power is how fast it is transferred, measured in watts (1 W = 1 J per second). Climbing the same staircase does the same work whether you sprint or stroll — only the power output differs. The calculator’s Power mode converts between work, power and time.

What units does work use, and how do I convert them?+

The SI unit is the joule: 1 J = 1 N·m. Common conversions: 1 kJ = 1,000 J, 1 cal = 4.184 J, 1 kcal = 4,184 J, 1 Wh = 3,600 J, 1 ft·lbf ≈ 1.356 J. The calculator converts every result into these units automatically.
