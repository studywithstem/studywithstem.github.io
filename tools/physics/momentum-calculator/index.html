03 · THE FORMULAS

Every formula behind the tool.

The six equations the calculator uses — the same ones shown with your numbers substituted in every result.

Momentum

p = m × v

Mass in kilograms times velocity in metres per second gives momentum in kg·m/s — a vector pointing the way the object moves.

Impulse

J = F × Δt

A force in newtons acting for a time in seconds. The impulse is the momentum change the force delivers, measured in N·s.

Impulse–momentum theorem

Δp = m(v2 − v1) = F × Δt

The same change in momentum seen from either side: a velocity change over time, or a force acting for a time.

Conservation of momentum

m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2

With no outside forces, total momentum before a collision equals total momentum after — always, elastic or not.

Perfectly inelastic collision

v = (m1u1 + m2u2) ÷ (m1 + m2)

The objects stick together and move at one speed — the momentum-weighted average of the two starting velocities.

Elastic collision (1D)

v1' = ((m1−m2)u1 + 2m2u2) ÷ (m1+m2)
v2' = ((m2−m1)u2 + 2m1u1) ÷ (m1+m2)

Both momentum and kinetic energy come out conserved — billiard-ball physics. Equal masses simply swap velocities.

04 · FAQ

Questions students actually ask.

Short, exam-ready answers. If yours isn’t here, the formula cards above probably cover it.

How do I calculate momentum?+

Multiply mass by velocity: p = m × v. A 1,200 kg car travelling at 25 m/s has p = 1,200 × 25 = 30,000 kg·m/s. Momentum is a vector — direction matters, so a minus sign means the opposite direction. Mode 01 solves for any missing variable and converts units for you.

What are the units of momentum?+

The SI unit is the kilogram metre per second (kg·m/s), numerically identical to the newton-second (N·s). Imperial uses lb·ft/s and CGS uses g·cm/s. The calculator accepts all of these and converts between them automatically.

What is the difference between momentum and impulse?+

Momentum is a property an object has at one moment (mass × velocity). Impulse is the change you make to it: a force acting over a time interval, J = F × Δt. The impulse–momentum theorem says they are numerically equal — J = Δp — so a 50 N push lasting 4 s changes momentum by 200 kg·m/s.

Can momentum be negative?+

Yes. Momentum is a vector, so its sign shows direction along a chosen axis. A car braking from +25 m/s to 0 m/s has Δp = m(0 − 25) = −30,000 kg·m/s — the minus just means the change points backwards. In collisions, enter negative velocities for objects moving the other way.

What does conservation of momentum mean?+

If no outside force acts on a system, its total momentum stays constant: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂. This holds for every collision — elastic or not — which is why the after-speeds can be predicted from the before-speeds. The Collisions tab checks it and shows momentum before and after.

What is the difference between elastic and inelastic collisions?+

Momentum is conserved in both. In a perfectly elastic collision kinetic energy is also conserved — objects bounce apart unchanged, like billiard balls. In an inelastic collision some kinetic energy becomes heat, sound or deformation; if the objects stick together and move as one, it’s perfectly inelastic. The calculator shows kinetic energy before and after so you can see exactly how much was lost.

How do I convert units before calculating?+

You don’t need to — pick any unit from the dropdowns and the calculator converts internally. If doing it by hand: km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s, mph × 0.44704 = m/s, ft/s × 0.3048 = m/s, lb × 0.45359237 = kg, tonne × 1000 = kg.

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
