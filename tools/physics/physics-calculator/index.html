03 · THE FORMULAS

Every formula it uses, on one card.

The same equations the calculator substitutes your numbers into — worth having in memory for exam day.

Equations of motion

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t

The SUVAT set. Each line uses a different three of s, u, v, a, t — so any three known values unlock the other two.

Newton's second law

F = m × a

Force (N) = mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s²). One newton is the push that speeds 1 kg up by 1 m/s each second.

Weight

W = m × g

Weight is a force, not a mass. The same 70 kg person: 686 N on Earth, 113 N on the Moon — the kg never change, the gravity does.

Mechanical energy

KE = ½mv²
PE = mgh

Kinetic is energy of motion (v is squared — double the speed, quadruple the energy); potential is energy of position, banked by height against gravity.

Work

W = F · d · cos θ

Energy transferred by a force over a distance. Push along the motion (0°) for full effect; sideways (90°) transfers nothing.

Ohm's law & power

V = I × R
P = V × I

Voltage equals current times resistance; power equals voltage times current. Two measurements pin down a whole circuit.

04 · FAQ

Physics questions, answered.

The eight things students ask most about motion, force, energy and Ohm's law — collected in one place.

How do I calculate final velocity?+

Use v = u + at — starting velocity u, plus acceleration a times time t. An object dropped from rest (u = 0) falling for 3 s at g = 9.81 m/s² reaches v = 0 + 9.81 × 3 = 29.43 m/s. The Motion tab solves this instantly and shows the distance covered too.

What are the SUVAT equations of motion?+

Four equations link the five quantities of constant-acceleration motion: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as and s = ½(u + v)t. Because each equation uses a different three of the five, knowing any three values is always enough to find the rest. Pick the quantity you want in the Motion tab and the calculator asks for exactly the three it needs.

How do I calculate force with Newton's second law?+

Multiply mass by acceleration: F = ma, with mass in kilograms and acceleration in metres per second squared, giving force in newtons. A 60 kg mass accelerating at 2 m/s² needs F = 60 × 2 = 120 N. One newton is defined exactly this way — the force that accelerates 1 kg at 1 m/s².

How do I calculate weight on the Moon or Mars?+

Weight is mass times local gravity: W = mg. Your mass never changes, but gravity does — 9.807 m/s² on Earth, 1.62 on the Moon and 3.721 on Mars. A 70 kg person weighs about 686 N on Earth, 113 N on the Moon and 260 N on Mars. The Force & weight tab has all these presets built in.

How do I calculate kinetic and potential energy?+

Kinetic energy is KE = ½mv² — a 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s carries ½ × 1000 × 20² = 200,000 J. Potential energy is PE = mgh — lifting 100 kg up 2 m on Earth stores 100 × 9.807 × 2 ≈ 1961 J. Work done by a force is W = F × d × cos θ, also in joules, ready to compare with both.

How do I use Ohm's law to find voltage, current or power?+

Ohm's law says V = I × R: voltage in volts equals current in amps times resistance in ohms. Rearrange it for whatever is missing — I = V ÷ R or R = V ÷ I. Power follows from P = V × I, so a 120 V bulb drawing 0.5 A uses 60 W and has 240 Ω of resistance. The Ohm's law tab solves for any of the four.

What units does the physics calculator use?+

SI units throughout: metres (m), seconds (s), metres per second (m/s), newtons (N), kilograms (kg), joules (J), volts (V), amps (A), ohms (Ω) and watts (W). For convenience, energy results are also converted to food calories and kilowatt-hours, and weight to pounds-force.

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
