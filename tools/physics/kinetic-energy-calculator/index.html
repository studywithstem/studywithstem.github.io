03 · THE FORMULA

One formula, three ways to use it.

Every mode above is the same equation, just solved for a different letter.

Kinetic energy

KE = ½ · m · v2

Mass in kilograms, velocity in metres per second, energy comes out in joules (kg·m²/s²).

Solving for velocity

v = √(2 · KE ÷ m)

Divide the kinetic energy by the mass, double it, then take the square root.

Solving for mass

m = 2 · KE ÷ v2

Double the kinetic energy and divide by the square of the velocity.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is the formula for kinetic energy?+

Kinetic energy is KE = ½ × m × v², where m is mass in kilograms and v is velocity in metres per second. The result comes out in joules. A 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s has KE = 0.5 × 2 × 3² = 9 J.

How do I calculate the kinetic energy of a moving object?+

Enter the object's mass and velocity in the Kinetic energy tab, in whatever units you have — kilograms or pounds, m/s or km/h. The calculator converts everything to kilograms and metres per second, applies the formula, and shows the answer in joules, kilojoules, calories, kWh, eV, ft·lb and BTU.

How do I find velocity from kinetic energy?+

Rearrange the formula: v = √(2 × KE ÷ m). Enter the known kinetic energy and mass in the Solve velocity tab and the calculator returns speed in m/s, km/h, mph, ft/s and knots.

How do I find mass from kinetic energy?+

Rearrange the formula: m = 2 × KE ÷ v². Enter the known kinetic energy and velocity in the Solve mass tab and the calculator returns mass in kilograms, grams, pounds and ounces.

What units is kinetic energy measured in?+

The SI unit is the joule (J), equal to 1 kg·m²/s². Other common units include the kilojoule, calorie, kilocalorie, kilowatt-hour, electronvolt, foot-pound and BTU. The Unit converter tab switches between all of them instantly.

Does kinetic energy depend on direction?+

No — kinetic energy is a scalar, not a vector. It only depends on speed, not direction. Because velocity is squared in the formula, moving backward at a given speed produces exactly the same kinetic energy as moving forward at that speed, and the result is always zero or positive.
