03 · READING THE DIAGRAM

What you're actually looking at.

Each arrow is a force — its length is the magnitude and its direction is the push or pull.

Gravity (weight)

W = m · g (always points down)

The amber arrow pulls the block toward the centre of the Earth. Its magnitude is the mass times the gravitational field strength, and it never disappears in a free-body diagram.

Normal force

N (perpendicular to the surface)

The teal arrow is the surface pushing back on the block. On a flat table it points straight up and usually balances gravity exactly when the block is not accelerating vertically.

Friction

f = μ · N (opposes sliding)

The amber-deep arrow always points opposite to the direction the block would slide. It is zero when there is no attempt to move, and grows until it reaches its maximum value.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is a force diagram?+

A force diagram — also called a free-body diagram — shows a single object isolated from its surroundings with every force acting on it drawn as an arrow. The length of each arrow is proportional to the size of the force and the arrow points in the direction the force pushes or pulls.

What is net force?+

Net force is the single force you get when you add all the individual force vectors together. If the net force is zero the object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity; if it is not zero the object accelerates in the direction of the net force according to F = ma.

What are the common forces on a block?+

The usual suspects are weight (gravity pulling down), normal force (the surface pushing up perpendicular to it), friction (opposing sliding along the surface), tension (a rope or string pulling), and any applied force you push or pull with.

How do you add forces that are not along the same line?+

You break each force into horizontal and vertical components, add the components separately, then combine the totals with the Pythagorean theorem. The direction of the net force comes from the inverse tangent of the vertical sum over the horizontal sum.

What happens when the net force is zero?+

When all forces balance the object is in equilibrium: it either stays still or keeps moving at a constant velocity in a straight line. There is no acceleration, which is why a book resting on a table does not sink or float.

Is this force diagram tool free and private?+

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you build or drag is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
