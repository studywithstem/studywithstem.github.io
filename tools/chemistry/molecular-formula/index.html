03 · THE MATH

How the formula is found.

Every calculation follows the same classic sequence.

Moles from percent

n = % ÷ A

Divide each element's percentage by its atomic mass to get moles (assuming 100 g of sample).

Simplest ratio

ratio = nᵢ ÷ nsmallest

Divide every mole value by the smallest one to get the empirical formula subscripts.

Molecular multiplier

k = M ÷ Mempirical

Divide the given molar mass by the empirical formula mass to get the whole-number multiplier.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is a molecular formula?+

A molecular formula shows the exact number of each type of atom in a molecule. For example, glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆ — six carbon, twelve hydrogen, and six oxygen atoms. It is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

What is the difference between empirical and molecular formula?+

The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound (e.g., CH₂O for glucose). The molecular formula is the actual number of atoms in a molecule (e.g., C₆H₁₂O₆). The molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

How do you find the molecular formula from percent composition?+

First convert the percent composition to moles by dividing each percentage by the element's atomic mass. Then divide all mole values by the smallest one to get the empirical formula. Finally, divide the given molar mass by the empirical formula mass to get the multiplier, and multiply the empirical formula by that number.

Can this calculator determine the molecular formula from mass data?+

Yes. Enter the mass of each element in grams and the total molar mass of the compound (if known). The calculator converts masses to moles, finds the simplest ratio, and determines the molecular formula automatically.

What if the molar mass is not known?+

If the molar mass is not provided, the calculator will still determine the empirical formula from the given composition. The molecular formula requires the molar mass to find the multiplier.

Is this tool free, and is my data sent anywhere?+

It is completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to a server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
