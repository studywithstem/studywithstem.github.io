03 · THE FORMULAS

Five laws, one family of ideas.

Each law fixes one property and relates the other two — the Ideal Gas Law ties all of them together.

Boyle's Law

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Constant temperature and amount of gas. Pressure and volume move in opposite directions.

Charles's Law

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Constant pressure and amount of gas. Volume rises and falls with absolute temperature.

Gay-Lussac's Law

P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂

Constant volume and amount of gas. Pressure rises and falls with absolute temperature.

Combined Gas Law

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

Constant amount of gas, everything else free to change. Boyle's, Charles's and Gay-Lussac's Laws are special cases of this one.

Ideal Gas Law

PV = nRT

R = 0.082057 L·atm/(mol·K). Relates pressure, volume, moles and temperature directly, with no before-and-after state.

Absolute temperature

K = °C + 273.15

Every gas law above needs temperature in kelvin — this calculator converts Celsius or Fahrenheit automatically before solving.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is Boyle's Law?+

Boyle's Law says that at constant temperature and amount of gas, pressure and volume are inversely related: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. Compress a gas into a smaller volume and its pressure rises by the same factor.

What is Charles's Law?+

Charles's Law says that at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂. Temperature has to be in kelvin for the ratio to hold.

What is Gay-Lussac's Law?+

Gay-Lussac's Law says that at constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature: P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂. Heating a sealed rigid container raises the pressure inside proportionally.

What is the Combined Gas Law?+

The Combined Gas Law merges Boyle's, Charles's and Gay-Lussac's Laws for a fixed amount of gas: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. Use it whenever pressure, volume and temperature all change at once.

What does PV = nRT mean?+

It's the Ideal Gas Law: P is pressure, V is volume, n is moles of gas, T is absolute temperature, and R is the gas constant (0.082057 L·atm per mol·K). It relates all four properties of a gas at once, without needing a before-and-after state.

Why do gas laws need kelvin instead of Celsius?+

Gas laws are ratios built on absolute temperature, where zero really means zero kinetic energy. Celsius and Fahrenheit have arbitrary zero points, so a ratio built on them gives the wrong answer. This calculator converts any temperature unit to kelvin automatically before solving.

Is this tool free, and where does my data go?+

It's completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to any server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
