03 · THE FORMULA

One relationship, four ways to use it.

Every mode above comes from the same handful of equations, just solved for a different letter.

Moles from mass

n = m ÷ M

Mass in grams divided by molar mass in g/mol gives the amount of substance in moles.

Mass from moles

m = n × M

Multiply the number of moles by the molar mass to get the mass in grams.

Molar mass

M = m ÷ n

Divide the mass of a sample by the number of moles it contains.

Molarity

C = n ÷ V

Moles of solute divided by litres of solution gives the concentration in mol/L.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is a mole in chemistry?+

A mole is the SI unit for amount of substance. One mole of anything contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number) — atoms, molecules, ions, or formula units. It's the bridge between the microscopic world of atoms and the measurable, weighable world of grams.

How do I calculate moles from grams?+

Divide the mass by the molar mass: n = m ÷ M. For example, 18 g of water divided by its molar mass of 18.02 g/mol gives almost exactly 1 mole. Enter both values in the Moles from mass tab to get the answer instantly, along with the particle count.

How do I find molar mass?+

Molar mass is mass divided by moles: M = m ÷ n. If you know how many grams and how many moles a sample contains, the Solve molar mass tab rearranges the formula for you. It can also be found by adding up the atomic masses in a chemical formula.

How do I calculate molarity?+

Molarity is moles of solute divided by litres of solution: C = n ÷ V. Enter the moles and the solution volume in the Molarity tab and it returns the concentration in mol/L (M), along with mmol/L and µmol/L.

How many particles are in a mole?+

Exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ particles, known as Avogadro's number. This holds regardless of the substance — one mole of carbon atoms and one mole of water molecules both contain the same number of particles, just with very different total masses.

What is the molar volume of a gas at STP?+

At standard temperature and pressure (0°C and 1 atm), one mole of any ideal gas occupies 22.414 L. The Moles from mass tab shows this figure automatically so you can see what volume a gas sample would occupy at STP.

Is this tool free, and where does my data go?+

It's completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to any server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
