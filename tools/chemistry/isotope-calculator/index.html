03 · THE FORMULA

Four ideas, one periodic table.

Every mode above comes from one of these core isotope relationships.

Average atomic mass

M = Σ (massi · fractioni)

Multiply each isotope's mass by its fractional abundance, then sum across all isotopes.

Two-isotope abundance

x = (M − m2) ÷ (m1 − m2)

Solve the weighted-average equation for the fractional abundance of the lighter isotope.

Atomic composition

n = A − Z  ·  e⁻ = Z − charge

Neutrons come from mass number minus atomic number; electrons come from atomic number minus charge.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

How do you calculate average atomic mass from isotopes?+

Multiply each isotope's mass by its fractional abundance (percent abundance ÷ 100), then add the results together. Chlorine is 75.76% Cl-35 (34.969 amu) and 24.24% Cl-37 (36.966 amu): (34.969 × 0.7576) + (36.966 × 0.2424) ≈ 35.45 amu, matching the periodic table value.

How do I find the percent abundance of two isotopes?+

For a two-isotope element, let x be the fractional abundance of the lighter isotope. Since the weighted average must equal the known atomic mass: M = m1·x + m2·(1 − x). Solving gives x = (M − m2) ÷ (m1 − m2). The Solve abundance tab does this and returns both percentages.

How do I find protons, neutrons and electrons from mass number and atomic number?+

The atomic number (Z) equals the number of protons, and in a neutral atom it also equals the number of electrons. Neutrons = mass number (A) minus atomic number: n = A − Z. For an ion, subtract the charge from Z to get electrons. Carbon-14 (Z=6, A=14) has 6 protons, 8 neutrons, and 6 electrons if neutral.

What's the difference between mass number and atomic mass?+

Mass number is a whole number — the total count of protons plus neutrons in one specific isotope, like 14 for carbon-14. Atomic mass is a decimal value, usually the abundance-weighted average of all naturally occurring isotopes of an element, like 12.011 for carbon on the periodic table.

How do I convert between mass, moles and number of atoms?+

moles = mass ÷ molar mass, and atoms = moles × Avogadro's number (6.02214076 × 10²³). Reverse the steps to go from atoms or moles back to mass. The Mass ↔ moles ↔ atoms tab handles the conversion either way.

Is this tool free, and where does my data go?+

It's completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent to any server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
