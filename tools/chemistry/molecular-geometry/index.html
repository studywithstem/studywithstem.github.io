03 · READING THE DIAGRAM

What you're actually looking at.

The model is drawn to make three real chemical facts easy to see, not just decorative motion.

The central atom

central atom (teal) + bonded atoms (amber)

The atom in the middle is where all the action happens. Its bonding pairs and lone pairs determine the entire shape of the molecule through VSEPR repulsion.

Lone pairs

lone pair (grey) — extra repulsion

Lone pairs are shown as fuzzy clouds because they occupy more space than bonding pairs. They squeeze bond angles smaller — which is why water is 104.5° instead of 109.5°.

Bond angles

electron domains → geometry → angle

The angle between bonded atoms is determined purely by how many electron domains surround the central atom. The readout panel shows the ideal angle for each geometry.

04 · FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What is VSEPR theory?+

VSEPR stands for Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion. It states that electron pairs around a central atom arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion. This determines the electron-pair geometry, and the molecular geometry is what you get after accounting for lone pairs.

How do lone pairs affect molecular shape?+

Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs because they are held by only one nucleus. This extra repulsion pushes bonding pairs closer together, reducing bond angles. For example, methane (CH₄) has 109.5° angles, but ammonia (NH₃) with one lone pair has 107°, and water (H₂O) with two lone pairs has 104.5°.

What are the main molecular geometries?+

The main geometries based on electron domains are: linear (2 domains), trigonal planar (3), tetrahedral (4), trigonal bipyramidal (5), and octahedral (6). When lone pairs are present, the molecular shape changes to bent, trigonal pyramidal, seesaw, T-shaped, or square pyramidal.

How do you determine hybridization?+

Hybridization is determined by the number of electron domains around the central atom: 2 domains = sp, 3 = sp², 4 = sp³, 5 = sp³d, and 6 = sp³d². This explains the geometry and bond angles observed in the molecule.

What is the difference between electron geometry and molecular geometry?+

Electron geometry includes all electron domains (bonding pairs and lone pairs) around the central atom. Molecular geometry describes only the positions of the atoms, ignoring lone pairs. For example, water has tetrahedral electron geometry but bent molecular geometry.

Why are bond angles important?+

Bond angles determine the 3D shape of a molecule, which in turn affects its physical properties, reactivity, and how it interacts with other molecules. Small changes in bond angles can dramatically change a molecule's behavior, especially in biological systems like enzymes and receptors.

Is this tool free, and where does my data go?+

It's completely free and runs entirely in your browser — nothing you build or click is uploaded, logged, or sent to any server. No account, no sign-up, no limits.
