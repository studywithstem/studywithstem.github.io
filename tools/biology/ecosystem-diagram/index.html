    03 · IN NUMBERS

    Ecosystems, measured.

    Energy and matter move through ecosystems in ways you can count — these are the numbers that matter.

    The 10% rule

    ≈10% ENERGY TRANSFER · 90% LOST AS HEAT

    Only about one-tenth of the energy at one trophic level makes it into the bodies of the next. The rest is lost to movement, heat and waste — which is why top predators are so few and food chains are rarely longer than four or five links.

    Trophic levels

    PRODUCERS → PRIMARY → SECONDARY → TERTIARY

    Producers capture sunlight. Primary consumers eat them. Secondary consumers eat the primary consumers. Tertiary consumers eat the secondary consumers. Decomposers feed on all levels.

    The carbon cycle in numbers

    ≈750 Gt C IN ATMOSPHERE · ≈560 Gt C IN BIOMASS

    Earth’s atmosphere holds roughly 750 gigatonnes of carbon. Living things store about 560 gigatonnes. Every year, photosynthesis and respiration move billions of tonnes between them.

    04 · QUESTIONS

    Things people ask.

    Everything about how the diagram works, how honest the drawing is, and what it saves (nothing).

    Still stuck? Write to us — we read everything.

    How do I use the interactive ecosystem diagram?+

    Drag anywhere on the diagram to rotate the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click any organism to open its role. Use the view chips to switch between food web, energy pyramid and nutrient cycle.

    What is a food web?+

    A food web is the network of feeding relationships in an ecosystem. Unlike a simple food chain, it shows every organism eating and being eaten by multiple others, which is how real ecosystems work.

    What is an energy pyramid?+

    An energy pyramid shows how much energy is available at each trophic level. Only about 10% of the energy at one level passes to the next — the rest is lost as heat, movement and waste. That's why there are far more producers than top predators.

    What is a nutrient cycle?+

    A nutrient cycle is the path an element — like carbon or nitrogen — takes through living things, the soil, the water and the air. Decomposers play a key role by returning nutrients to the soil so producers can use them again.

    Why do arrows point from prey to predator?+

    Arrows in a food web show the direction energy flows — from the organism being eaten to the one that eats it. So an arrow from a rabbit to a fox means the fox gains energy by eating the rabbit.

    What are producers, consumers and decomposers?+

    Producers (plants, algae) make their own food from sunlight. Consumers (animals) eat other organisms. Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead material and return nutrients to the soil.

    Does the tool save or upload anything?+

    No. The entire diagram runs in your browser. Nothing you click, zoom or select is ever sent to a server, logged, or stored.

    Can I use this on my phone or tablet?+

    Yes. One finger rotates the diagram, pinching zooms, and tapping selects — so the whole tool works the same with touch as it does with a mouse.

    Is this interactive ecosystem diagram free to use?+

    Yes, it's completely free with no account, no sign-up, and no limit on how much you explore.
