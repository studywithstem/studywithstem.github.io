    FIELD GUIDE

    The parts worth knowing

    Six cheat-sheet cards for the structures examiners love to ask about. All of them are clickable in the diagram above.

    Chromosomes

    1 chromosome = 1 DNA molecule

    Loose chromatin in interphase, condensed X-shapes by metaphase. Each X is two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere — here drawn in teal and amber.

    The spindle

    3 fiber types · 1 machine

    Kinetochore fibers pull chromosomes apart, polar fibers elongate the cell, astral fibers brace the spindle against the membrane and set the division axis.

    Centrosome

    2 per cell · 2 centrioles each

    The microtubule-organising centre. It duplicates in interphase, its two copies become the spindle poles, and each daughter cell inherits one.

    Nuclear envelope

    breaks · recycles · rebuilds

    A double membrane that shatters into vesicles so the spindle can reach the DNA — then those same vesicles reassemble as the two daughter envelopes.

    Kinetochore

    the checkpoint sensor

    The protein platform on each centromere. It only green-lights anaphase when every chromatid feels equal tension from both poles — the famous spindle checkpoint.

    Cleavage furrow

    actin + myosin drawstring

    A contractile ring of the same proteins that power your muscles, tightening beneath the membrane until one cell is pinched into two.

    8 QUESTIONS · NO JARGON

    What are the stages of mitosis in order?+

    The classic order is prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase — remember it as PMAT — with cytokinesis overlapping telophase to finish the split. Interphase comes before mitosis and is where the cell copies its DNA. The full sequence: I-PMAT-C.

    Is interphase part of mitosis?+

    No. Mitosis strictly means division of the nucleus — the PMAT stages. Interphase is the growth and copying phase where DNA replication happens; without it, mitosis would have nothing to divide. Cytokinesis, the division of the cytoplasm, is also technically a separate process.

    When do sister chromatids separate?+

    In anaphase. Once every chromosome is properly attached to the spindle, the centromeres split and each sister chromatid is pulled toward the opposite pole. From that moment on, each former chromatid counts as a full chromosome in its own right.

    What do spindle fibers actually do?+

    They run three different jobs. Kinetochore fibers attach to chromosomes and shorten to pull the chromatids apart. Polar fibers from opposite poles overlap in the middle and slide against each other to elongate the cell. Astral fibers brace the spindle against the membrane and set the division axis.

    How is this diagram simplified compared to a real cell?+

    This model shows 4 chromosomes, while a human cell has 46. Real interphase lasts hours and takes roughly 90% of the cell cycle, and the real stages blend into each other rather than following a clean timeline. The sequence and mechanics are faithful; the count and timescale are simplified for clarity.

    What is the difference between mitosis and meiosis?+

    Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid cells for growth and repair. Meiosis produces four genetically different haploid gametes — it shuffles DNA through crossing over and divides twice instead of once.

    What is the cleavage furrow made of?+

    A contractile ring of actin and myosin — the same protein duo that powers your muscles — tightening like a drawstring beneath the membrane until the cell pinches into two.
