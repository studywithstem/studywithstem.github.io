03 — THE SIGNAL'S JOURNEY

How a neuron fires, end to end

This is exactly what you're watching when you press Fire Impulse — the six stages of a single message crossing one neuron.

1 · Receive

inputs → mV-scale blips

Neurotransmitters land on the dendrites, opening ion channels and starting tiny local voltage shifts called graded potentials.

2 · Integrate

Σ excite + Σ inhibit → V

The cell body sums every excitatory and inhibitory input over a few milliseconds. Loud voices, quiet voices — all counted.

3 · Decide

threshold ≈ −55 mV

At the axon hillock the verdict lands: if the summed voltage crosses threshold, voltage-gated sodium channels snap open. No half-firing.

4 · Fire

−70 mV → +30 mV

Sodium rushes in and the membrane voltage flips — the action potential, an all-or-nothing spike that never travels at half strength.

5 · Leap

speed up to ~120 m/s

The spike regenerates only at the nodes of Ranvier, leaping between them — saltatory conduction, myelin's whole point.

6 · Deliver

cleft ≈ 20–40 nm

At the terminal, calcium triggers vesicles to fuse with the membrane and spill neurotransmitters across the synapse to the next cell.

05 — THREE JOBS

Sensory, motor, and everything between

Neurons come in many shapes, but nearly all of them fall into one of three jobs. The one in the diagram is drawn as the long-range kind.

Sensory (afferent)

SENSE → SPINAL CORD

Carry messages from your skin, eyes, ears and every other sensor toward the brain and spinal cord. Built long and myelinated for speed — the diagram above is one of these.

Motor (efferent)

SPINAL CORD → MUSCLE

Carry commands outward — to muscles, glands and organs. Their single axons can run a full metre from spine to toe without a single break.

Interneuron

NEURON → NEURON

The connective tissue of thought: short, dense, wildly branched, linking neurons to other neurons. Over 99% of the neurons in your nervous system are these.

06 — QUESTIONS

Neuron diagram FAQ

Everything people ask about exploring the neuron — from how exact the diagram is, to why myelin has holes in it, to what that Fire button really does.

Tap a question to unfold it.

How do I use the interactive neuron diagram?+

Hover any structure to preview its name, then click it to open its dossier — what it does, plus a quick fact. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and press Fire Impulse to watch an action potential travel the full neuron. The quick-select list on the right jumps straight to any part.

Is this diagram anatomically exact?+

No. It is a stylized illustration built for clarity: a real motor neuron has far denser branching, thousands of synapses, and proportions that vary with its job. Every labelled structure is real and sits correctly along the signal's path — the shapes are simplified so each part stays easy to spot, click, and tell apart.

What does the Fire Impulse button actually show?+

A full action potential journey. The signal sweeps in through the dendrites, is summed at the cell body, crosses threshold at the axon hillock, leaps between the nodes of Ranvier in saltatory conduction, and arrives at the terminal — where vesicles release neurotransmitter molecules across the synaptic cleft to the next cell.

Why does the myelin sheath have gaps in it?+

Those gaps are the nodes of Ranvier. Myelin insulates the axon, so the electrical signal can only regenerate at these bare nodes — and regenerating node by node lets it travel up to a hundred times faster than it could crawl along an uninsulated membrane.

How fast do real neurons carry signals?+

It depends on thickness and insulation. Thin unmyelinated fibres manage roughly 0.5 to 2 metres per second, while thick myelinated ones reach 100 to 120 metres per second. That difference is why you snatch your hand off a hot pan before your brain has finished feeling it.

What is the difference between dendrites and the axon?+

Dendrites are the many-branched inputs: they receive signals and carry them toward the cell body. A neuron has exactly one axon, its output — a single fibre that carries the signal away to the terminals. Many voices in, one voice out.

Does the tool save or upload anything?+

No. The whole diagram runs in your browser. Nothing you click, zoom, or fire is ever sent to a server, logged, or stored.

Is this interactive neuron diagram free to use?+

Yes. It is completely free with no account, no sign-up, and no limit on how much you explore — on desktop, tablet, and phone alike.
