How Does Trigonometry Work?

Sine, cosine and tangent — the three ratios that connect angles to sides, explained with the unit circle, SOHCAHTOA and a live interactive triangle.

8 MIN READ · INTERACTIVE DIAGRAM INSIDE

Any time you tilt a ladder, aim a telescope, or check how steep a hill is, you're doing trigonometry in your head. Here's the simple machinery underneath.

Trigonometry is the branch of mathematics that connects angles to side lengths. Its three main characters — sine, cosine and tangent — are just ratios that compare the sides of a right‑angled triangle. Once you see them as ratios, everything from wave graphs to GPS calculations starts to make sense.

SINE

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse

Compares the side opposite the angle to the longest side.

COSINE

cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

Compares the side next to the angle to the longest side.

TANGENT

tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Compares the two shorter sides — the slope of the angle.

The Right Triangle Setup

SIDES & ANGLES

Every right triangle has one 90° corner and two acute angles. Pick one of those acute angles and call it θ (theta). The three sides get special names relative to θ:

  • Hypotenuse — the longest side, always opposite the 90° angle.
  • Opposite — the side across from θ.
  • Adjacent — the side next to θ that isn't the hypotenuse.

These labels flip if you choose the other acute angle, which is why it's important to always mark which angle you're working from.

Quick check

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is always the same side no matter which acute angle you pick — but “opposite” and “adjacent” swap when you switch angles.

SOHCAHTOA: The Three Ratios

MEMORY AID

The word SOHCAHTOA packs all three definitions into one string. Each group of three letters tells you which sides to divide:

sin θ = O / H Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse
cos θ = A / H Cosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse
tan θ = O / A Tangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent

Because the hypotenuse is always the longest side, sine and cosine are always between 0 and 1 (for acute angles). Tangent can be larger than 1 — it blows up as the angle approaches 90°.

Worked example

A right triangle has an angle θ = 30°, and the hypotenuse is 10 cm. How long is the opposite side?

  1. We know the angle and hypotenuse, and we want the opposite side — that's the sine ratio.
  2. Write it: sin 30° = opposite / 10
  3. Multiply both sides by 10: opposite = 10 × sin 30°
  4. Since sin 30° = 0.5: opposite = 5 cm

The Unit Circle: Beyond Right Triangles

ANGLES EVERYWHERE

Right triangles only handle angles between 0° and 90°. To talk about any angle — 120°, 200°, even negative angles — mathematicians use the unit circle.

Picture a circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. Start at the point (1, 0) and rotate anticlockwise by an angle θ. The coordinates of the point you land on are (cos θ, sin θ). That's the whole idea: cosine is the horizontal coordinate, sine is the vertical coordinate.

This extends the definitions naturally to all angles and makes the repeating wave patterns of sine and cosine visible — they oscillate between −1 and 1 forever.

Why it matters

The unit circle turns trigonometry from a triangle‑only tool into a language for rotation, waves, and anything that repeats — sound, light, alternating current, planetary orbits.

Where Trigonometry Shows Up in Real Life

Trigonometry is not an abstract exercise — it's the maths of anything involving angles and distances.

  • Surveying & navigation: finding the height of a mountain or the distance to a ship from a known baseline.
  • Architecture & engineering: calculating roof slopes, ramp angles, and the forces in a bridge truss.
  • Computer graphics & games: rotating objects in 2D and 3D, and simulating light and shadows.
  • Sound & electricity: sine waves describe alternating current, sound waves, and musical tones.
  • Astronomy: measuring distances to stars using parallax angles and the sine rule.
One formula to connect them all

Tangent is just sine divided by cosine: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. And the Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 falls straight out of the unit circle's radius‑1 definition.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

The short answers — with the long ones one tap away.

What are sine, cosine and tangent in simple words?+

They are three ratios that compare the sides of a right triangle. Sine compares the opposite side to the hypotenuse, cosine compares the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, and tangent compares the opposite side to the adjacent side.

What does SOHCAHTOA mean?+

SOHCAHTOA is a memory aid: Sine = Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent over Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite over Adjacent. It helps you recall which sides go into each ratio.

Why does the unit circle matter in trigonometry?+

The unit circle is a circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. A point on the circle at angle θ has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ). This extends sine and cosine beyond right triangles to all angles, and makes their repeating wave patterns visible.

What is the formula for tangent?+

Tangent equals sine divided by cosine: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. In a right triangle it also equals the opposite side divided by the adjacent side.

Where is trigonometry used in real life?+

Trigonometry is used in navigation, surveying, architecture, engineering, computer graphics, music and astronomy. Any time you need to relate angles to distances — like finding the height of a building from its shadow — trigonometry is the tool.

What are the sine and cosine values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°?+

At 0°: sin = 0, cos = 1. At 30°: sin = 0.5, cos ≈ 0.866. At 45°: sin = cos ≈ 0.707. At 60°: sin ≈ 0.866, cos = 0.5. At 90°: sin = 1, cos = 0.
